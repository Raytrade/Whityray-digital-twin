# Ijaw Language & Community Atlas

This atlas is a research map, not a declaration that all listed communities speak the same dialect. Names and classifications must be reviewed against reliable linguistic sources and community knowledge before being used in product-facing claims.

## Collection rule

For every word, phrase, recording or proverb, capture:
- self-identified community of the speaker;
- language/variety name as the speaker uses it;
- town or area only at the level the speaker consents to share;
- speaker's age range only if relevant and voluntarily provided;
- context (home, market, waterside, ceremony, youth conversation, etc.);
- date and collector;
- whether the form is local, borrowed, mixed or uncertain.

Do not automatically label Kalabari, Okrika, Ibani, Nembe or any other named variety as “standard Izon”. Preserve the speaker's own label.

## Conversation domains to research

1. Greetings and leave-taking
2. Respectful speech with elders
3. Family and kinship
4. Food, cooking and hospitality
5. Market and bargaining
6. Fishing, boats, nets, tides and weather
7. Village and community life
8. Friendship, humour and teasing
9. Love, marriage and relationships
10. Disagreement, apology and reconciliation
11. Work, money and everyday hustle
12. Celebration, mourning and support
13. Traditional knowledge and oral history
14. Music, dance, performance and call-response
15. Modern youth speech and code-switching

## Dialect integrity

A word recorded in one community must remain tagged to that community until evidence supports a wider distribution. Similar-looking forms may have different meanings or pronunciation. Do not normalize spelling without recording the original form and the reason for any normalized form.
